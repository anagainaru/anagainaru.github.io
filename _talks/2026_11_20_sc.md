Scientific AI agents increasingly assist researchers in software development, workflow orchestration, 
data analysis, and simulation-driven discovery. However, scientific environments impose requirements 
beyond those of general-purpose assistants, including domain-specific knowledge integration, reproducibility, 
correctness verification, and continuous adaptation to evolving software ecosystems. In this paper, 
we present a design of scientific agents evaluated across four nuclear-fuel specialists spanning 
open-ended research to deterministic workflow execution. 

We evaluate the impact of structured knowledge 
vaults and reusable skills on agent performance, execution cost, and reliability and find that across 
five foundation models, the answer quality improves by roughly 1.2x-1.5x more than they raise execution cost. 
We describe a correctness-verification agent that traces cited references to validate outputs, 
reducing relative grading error against manual evaluation and discuss a path toward collaborative 
multi-agent systems combining agents that follow our design. The study provides practical guidance 
for building scalable, trustworthy scientific AI agents.
